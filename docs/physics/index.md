## Official Course Syllabus

| Module | Title | Prescribed Syllabus Content |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Statics of Particles** | Introduction to Newtonian mechanics, Vector mechanics, Forces on a particle, Parallelogram law, Resolution of a force, Rectangular components of a force, Resultant of several concurrent forces, Equilibrium of a particle, Free-body diagrams, Forces in space, Equilibrium of a particle in space. |

| **4** | **Kinetics of Particles: Energy Methods** | Work-energy and power, Work of a force, Conservative and non-conservative forces, Kinetic energy, Principle of work and energy, Potential energy, Conservation of energy, Power and efficiency, Solving problems using the concepts of friction wherever applicable. |
| **5** | **Kinetics of Particles: Momentum Methods** | Concept of conservation of momentum, Coefficient of restitution, Impulse-momentum principle, Impact: Direct central impact, Oblique central impact, Problems involving impulse and momentum. |

## The S-M-A-R&T Problem-Solving Protocol

To cultivate professional engineering problem-solving habits, all numerical examples throughout this course adhere to the **S-M-A-R&T** blueprint:

```
[S] Strategy  ──► Identify knowns, coordinate systems, and physical governing laws
      │
[M] Modeling  ──► Execute Boundary Tracing Protocol & draw Free-Body Diagram (FBD)
      │
[A] Analysis  ──► Apply algebraic equations (ΣF = 0, ΣF = ma, T₁ + U₁₋₂ = T₂, Imp₁₋₂ = Δp)
      │
[R] Reflect   ──► Check dimensional units, sign conventions, physical limits (T ≥ 0, 0 ≤ e ≤ 1)
      │
[T] Think     ──► Connect to aerospace, robotics, PINNs, and real-world machine applications
```

### Protocol Steps Breakdown
1. **Strategy (S):** Read the problem actively. State what is given in standard SI units and clearly declare the target unknowns.
2. **Modeling (M):** Draw a clear **Free-Body Diagram (FBD)**. Isolate the body with an imaginary boundary line. Replace every support, cable, contact surface, or spring crossing the boundary with its respective reaction force vector.
3. **Analysis (A):** Select optimal coordinate axes ($x\text{–}y$, normal-tangential $n\text{–}t$, or polar $r\text{–}\theta$). Write down governing scalar equations and solve systematically.
4. **Reflect (R):** Sanity check answers against physical intuition. Do ropes have positive tension? Does velocity decrease when braking? Are units dimensionally verified?
5. **Think Further (T):** Contrast idealized models with real machine constraints (thermal dissipation, Coulomb stick-slip, sensor feedback).
